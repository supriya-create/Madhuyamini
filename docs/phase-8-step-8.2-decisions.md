## Q1. Exchange preference at settlement

**Proposed behaviour.**

- Until step 8.3 ships replacement orders, the request form does not offer "exchange" (policy flag `exchanges.offered = false`; D8.4 stays approved and is honoured by 8.3). So in 8.2 no new exchange preference can be recorded.
- Any item that nevertheless carries an exchange preference (none exist today; the rule protects the future) **cannot be settled as a refund without the customer's explicit confirmation.** Settlement refuses with "customer choice needed". There is **no ADMIN override** that converts the preference, at any age.
- The customer's choice is an action on their return page, offered only after inspection: "Your replacement is not available. Choose: refund this item, or wait for a replacement." The choice is stored on the item with a timestamp and the actor; it is reversible until settlement; the e-mail `return.choice_needed` links to it. A "wait" choice is equally explicit and keeps the item open.
- **Unresponsive customer.** Reminders at 3, 7 and 14 days (engineering defaults). After 30 days without a choice the request is marked "on hold" (open, no clock, visible in the queue under its own tab) and the owner is alerted once. The remedies are human: the owner contacts the customer; when step 8.3 exists and stock returns, the owner fulfils the exchange the customer asked for. The restocked unit is already on the shelf since inspection; the shop holds the customer's money as an open obligation shown on the Operations page. Nothing closes the item as a refund without the customer's own confirmation. If the owner judges a case abandoned after a long period, that is a business write-off handled outside the system and recorded as a staff note, never as a refund the customer did not choose.

**Scenario.** Priya asks to exchange a medium kurta for a large. At inspection the large is sold out. She is e-mailed and chooses "refund this item" on her return page; the ADMIN then settles the refund of the medium's value. Had she chosen "wait", the item stays open until a large is back and 8.3 can ship it.

**Finance.** No effect on amounts; a confirmed refund is the item's value under the normal rules.

**Implications.** Security: owner-scoped, rate-limited customer action; the choice token is the signed-in session, nothing in e-mails. Operations: one more sub-state in the queue; an on-hold tab. Customer: money never replaces goods without their say.

**Recommendation.** Approve as revised.

**Status.** Approved by the owner on 3 November 2026, subject to the documented safeguards.

## Q3. Deductions: reasons, combination, ceiling and dispute

**Reasons (fixed list).**

| Reason                | Meaning                                          | Extra validation                              |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |

| `PACKAGING_DESTROYED` | Original packaging needed for resale is unusable | —                                             |
| `OTHER`               | Anything else                                    | Note of at least 10 characters                |

**Combination and ceiling.** A line may carry several deductions, one per reason, each with its own amount and at least one staff photograph from this request's inspection uploads. The **combined** deductions on a line may not exceed `floor(G_i × 30 ÷ 100)`, where `G_i` is the value of the line's accepted units (next paragraph); each deduction is at least 1 paisa; the same reason cannot appear twice on a line. Deductions apply to the line's accepted units as a whole (units are of one variant and one condition record), so the per-unit ceiling is the line ceiling divided across them. No deduction on any fault reason (damage on arrival, wrong item, not as described: the database refuses the first two, the service all three), none on a line whose write-off carries a `SHOP_FAULT` determination (Q8), and none when the condition is "as new".

**Deduction base (the owner's clarification of 12 November 2026).** The base of the ceiling is `G_i` from §4.3 of the proposal: the value of the line's accepted units after the proportional allocation of any order-level discount, that is `value(u + k) − value(u)` over `discounted_i` as defined in the arithmetic section below. Nothing else is in it: not the undiscounted line price, not the original shipping charge, not the cash-on-delivery fee, not the other lines of the order and not the order total. A deduction that would fit under any of those wider bases but not under `G_i` is refused (example 13). The ceiling is computed per line from the same figures the refund is computed from, so a coupon lowers the ceiling exactly as it lowers the refund.

## Q5. Bank-detail encryption, retention and key rotation

**Storage.** Bank details are collected in the return form for COD orders (D8.8), encrypted with AES-256-GCM under `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY` before the row is written, and stored as ciphertext plus the last four characters and the key version. Nothing displays more than the last four characters except an audited reveal (Q7).

**Retention and deletion.** The daily sweep nulls the ciphertext 30 days after the manual refund is marked paid, or 30 days after the request closes without a refund. The last four characters, key version and payment reference remain. Each deletion writes a system audit row (`customer_data.swept`, target `manual_refund` or `return`).

**Key rotation, concrete procedure (runbook, step 8.2 ships the tooling).**

1. _Generate_ a new 32-byte key; store it in the secrets manager with its creation date.
2. _Stage_: set `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY` to the new key and `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY_PREVIOUS` to the old one; deploy. From this moment new writes use the new key (version n+1) and reads accept both.
3. _Re-encrypt_: run `npm run job -- rotate-customer-data-key` (also schedulable). It selects rows whose `beneficiary_key_version < n+1` in batches of 100, decrypts with the matching key, re-encrypts under the current key, writes ciphertext and version in one conditional update per row (version unchanged since read), and is idempotent and resumable. It reports counts and writes one system audit row per run with totals; a row that fails to decrypt is counted, left unchanged and listed by id for investigation.
4. _Verify_: `SELECT count(*) … WHERE beneficiary_key_version < n+1` on both tables must be 0; the job's final run reports zero candidates; a sample reveal under the new key succeeds.
5. _Retire_: remove `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY_PREVIOUS` and deploy. The application no longer accepts the old key.
6. _Backups_: database backups taken before step 4 contain ciphertext under the old key. Keep the retired key sealed in the secrets manager, access-logged, for as long as any restorable backup holds such rows (the backup retention window chosen in Phase 11); then destroy it and record the destruction date. A restore from an older backup therefore requires the matching retired key, which the runbook states.
7. _Compromise_: if a key is suspected compromised, rotate at once (steps 1–5) and additionally run the retention sweep early for rows already paid.

**Scenario.** Meera's COD return is paid on 10 November; on 10 December her account number is gone from the database while the record shows "paid, account ending 4521, reference NEFT-…". A key rotation in between re-encrypted her row within minutes, invisible to her.

**Implications.** Data minimisation; the rotation tooling is small; a lost key makes details unrecoverable, hence the backup rule.

**Recommendation.** Approve 30 days and the procedure.

**Status.** Approved by the owner on 3 November 2026, subject to the documented safeguards.

## Q7. Access to full bank details

**Proposed behaviour (revised: no self-granting).**

- A **dedicated capability `returns.bank_view`** is required to reveal full bank details. No role holds it. It exists only through a **`bank` grant**: a row in the existing grant table with `fields = ['bank']`, scoped to **one return request** (`return_request_id` set; never "any customer"), for a stated reason, for at most 24 hours (the existing SQL ceiling; the form defaults to 2 hours).
- **Who may issue it: `returns.bank_grant`**, a sensitive capability held by the owner (ADMIN role) and by **grant managers**: staff accounts designated out of band with `npm run admin:grant-manager -- <email>` (the same shell-only, audited pattern as `admin:promote`; it requires a verified, enrolled account and writes a `script:` audit row; `--remove` clears the designation and revokes every bank grant that person issued). The designation is a column on the user (`users.grant_manager_since`), read live by `requirePermission` exactly like the role. The grant form offers only enrolled, verified staff other than the issuer as grantees.
- **Never to oneself.** The service refuses a grant whose grantee is the issuer, and the database enforces it: CHECK `NOT ('bank' = ANY(fields)) OR granted_by_id <> grantee_id`. Consequence, stated plainly: **revealing bank details always involves two people**, the one who authorises and the one who looks. A one-person shop must designate a second trusted account as grant manager (or as grantee) before any cash-on-delivery refund can be paid from revealed details.
- **Revocation.** The issuer, any grant manager or any ADMIN may revoke a grant at any time; revocation is immediate and ends any live reveal on the next render (the existing re-read rule). A role change or the removal of a designation revokes the bank grants that person holds and the ones they issued.
- **Expiry.** `expires_at` is checked on every reveal and every render; the daily sweep writes one audit row per grant when it lapses, with the number of reveals it served, so the log shows each grant's whole life.
- **Reveal.** Requires an active `bank` grant for this user and this request, a fresh second factor (10 minutes), the explicit "reveal" action with a reason, and is counted against `revealPerActor`. It writes a `staff_pii_reveals` row (`fields = ['bank']`, grant id, 10-minute expiry) and an audit row. The page renders the last four characters by default and never embeds the full value before the reveal. "Mark paid" and "cancel" keep their own capability (`returns.manual_refund`, ADMIN, sensitive); paying does not require viewing.

**Where bank details never appear (the owner's rule of 12 November 2026).** Full bank details exist in exactly two places: the ciphertext column and the response of an audited reveal. Everywhere else shows the last four characters at most: the admin queue, request and customer pages; the customer's own return page; the Operations and reports pages; the audit log (the `redactForAudit` allow-lists exclude the beneficiary fields and every free-text field that could carry them); the outbox payloads and every e-mail template, to the customer or to the owner; application and job logs, which name ids only; and any export (the admin has none; a future one would read the same DTOs, which never carry the ciphertext or the full number). A test seeds a COD return with a known account number and proves that none of these surfaces contains it (plan, criterion 6).

## Arithmetic: deterministic per-unit allocation and partial returns

**Allocation of an order-level discount to lines.** `share_i = floor(D × L_i / S)`; the remainder `D − Σ share_i` paise go one each to the lines with the largest fractional parts of `D × L_i / S`, ties broken by ascending variant id. `discounted_i = L_i − share_i`; the shares sum exactly to `D`.

**Per-unit values within a line.** For a line of `q` units with `discounted` value: `unit = floor(discounted / q)`, `r = discounted − unit × q` (0 ≤ r < q). Units are numbered 1..q; units 1..(q − r) are worth `unit`, units (q − r + 1)..q are worth `unit + 1`. So `value(k)`, the worth of the first `k` units, is `k × unit + max(0, k − (q − r))`, and `value(q) = discounted`.

**Which units a partial return takes.** Returned units of a line are always valued from unit 1 upward, continuing from where earlier settlements on the same line stopped: if `u` units of the line were already refunded (sum of `units` on earlier `ITEM_VALUE` lines for that order item), a settlement of `k` more units is worth `value(u + k) − value(u)`. The customer therefore never receives more than the average per unit on a partial return, and the sum of any sequence of partial returns equals the line's full discounted value once all `q` units are refunded. Deductions and caps apply after this value.

**Tests (step 8.2).** For every `q` in 1..10, every `r` in 0..q−1 and every `k` in 1..q: `value(q)` equals `discounted`; `value` is non-decreasing with increments of `unit` or `unit + 1` only; for every split of `q` into sequential partial returns, the sum of their values equals `discounted`; allocation shares sum to `D` and ties resolve by variant id; Appendix A vectors; property-style loops over random line sets.

## Q8. Repaired, quarantined and written-off units

**What the approved proposal says.** §4.3 counts restocked, repaired and quarantined units as accepted and refunds them at settlement, before repair or quarantine reaches its outcome, and refunds a written-off unit when "the fault is the shop's".

**The owner's rule (12 November 2026).** Three decisions are recorded separately, and none implies another:

**The outcomes, side by side.** Every unit of a return ends in exactly one of these. **The refund decision is derived, never selected.** The resolution form and `resolveHeldUnit` take only the disposition, the fault determination (required for a write-off, refused otherwise) and a reason. The service computes `refund_decision` from the table above (`REFUND_POLICY` or `NO_REFUND`) and writes it; the database CHECKs refuse any row in which disposition, fault determination and refund decision disagree, so neither a form, a replay nor direct SQL can record a refund inconsistent with the other two facts. ADMIN has no refund field to fill in. The same holds at inspection: the written-off line's fault determination decides, and the settlement computes.

**Goodwill refund: a separate decision.** The only way to pay a unit that policy does not pay is `recordGoodwillRefund`: a distinct sensitive ADMIN action with a fresh second factor, a reason of at least 20 characters, its own audit event `return.goodwill_refund`, its own return event and its own customer e-mail. It is allowed only on a non-fault write-off (`WRITTEN_OFF` with `CUSTOMER_FAULT` or `NO_FAULT`, at inspection or at a resolution), at most once per held unit or per written-off line (naming 1 to `write_off_qty` units), never on a send-back, never on a `SHOP_FAULT` unit (policy already pays), never above the unit's value and never with a deduction. It records `goodwill_refund_at`, `goodwill_refund_by_id` and `goodwill_reason` beside, not instead of, the derived `refund_decision = NO_REFUND`, so the audit log always shows both that policy refused and who overrode it and why. **Held units: repair and quarantine.** Units decided `REPAIR` or `QUARANTINE` at inspection are held out of stock (proposal §9.2, `held_qty`), and each becomes a row in `return_held_units` with its kind, a due date (`heldUnits.decisionDays`, 7) and a hard deadline (`heldUnits.deadlineDays`, 14). **Bound, checked by script.** The transitions are exactly: `HELD → OVERDUE` (job, due date), `OVERDUE → RESOLUTION_REQUIRED` (job, deadline), `OVERDUE` or `RESOLUTION_REQUIRED` `→ EXCEPTION` (ADMIN, once per unit), `EXCEPTION → RESOLUTION_REQUIRED` (job, at `exception_until`), any non-terminal state `→ RESOLVED` (ADMIN), and the single quarantine-to-repair re-disposition `→ HELD` with fresh dates. `RESOLVED` is terminal. The latest day on which the clock job can still change a unit's state is day 44 after its inspection (14 + 30), or day 58 for a quarantined unit re-dispositioned to repair at its deadline; after that the state is `RESOLUTION_REQUIRED` with an escalated daily alert until a person resolves it. The system therefore never makes a financial decision about a held unit; what it guarantees is that every state is named, visible and alerted, that the alerts escalate at day 7 and day 14, that the exception is bounded in length and in count, and that the only way out of `RESOLUTION_REQUIRED` is a human decision recorded with its disposition and fault determination, from which the refund decision follows. Revision 3's automatic `TIMED_OUT` state and `QUARANTINE_TIMEOUT` write-off are withdrawn.

**The day-14 customer notice is administrative only.** `return.held_delayed` says that the check (or repair) of the named unit is taking longer than the date the customer was given, gives the new expected date when an exception exists or says that staff will e-mail when it is resolved, and gives the support contact. It contains no amount, no deduction, no refund figure and no words of acceptance or approval; it does not change the proposal lifecycle (Q3), does not unlock or perform a settlement, and is not consent to anything by either side. The template test asserts that it carries no currency amount and none of the words "accepted", "approved", "refund of" and "deduction". The customer page shows the same wording.

**No duplicate refund for a late outcome.** A held unit is paid at most once, by exactly one of two paths, chosen under the `orders → return_requests` locks at the moment of resolution: if the request is not yet settled, the resolution only records the outcome and the next settlement pays the unit (its `ITEM_VALUE` refund line names the held unit); if the request is already settled, the resolution itself queues the unit's refund under the derived key `held:<heldUnitId>` (a goodwill decision under `goodwill:<heldUnitId>`). Four guards make a second payment impossible: the resolution is a conditional update from an unresolved state, and a repeated call returns the stored result instead of acting; `refunds.operation_key` is unique and the derived key is deterministic, so `queueRefundIntent` replays rather than inserts; `return_refund_lines` has a unique index on the held unit for `ITEM_VALUE` lines, so a unit cannot be on a settlement line and a resolution line at once; and a cash-on-delivery unit is added to the still-pending manual refund by a conditional update or, if that was already paid, to a new manual refund row under the same derived key, never both. A repair outcome recorded twice, a request retried after a crash, a resolution racing a settlement: each pays once (plan, criterion 11; example 15).

**What stays pending during a hold.** The held unit's item value is pending until its explicit resolution; nothing is paid for it before. The other accepted units of the same request are refunded at settlement, not delayed; a request whose only unit is held settles to zero with an explanatory line and closes. A held unit resolved **before** settlement is included at settlement at its full value (a deduction on it goes through a proposal like any other); one resolved **after** settlement is paid, if policy or a goodwill decision pays it, by the late-outcome path above. The original shipping charge is refunded at the first settlement or resolution at which D8.6's condition is met: at once when the shop is at fault (a `SHOP_FAULT` determination on any unit, or a confirmed fault reason), otherwise only when every unit of the order is accepted by policy (the outcomes table; a goodwill refund does not count), so a held unit defers the shipping refund exactly when that condition cannot be decided without it, and a unit sent back or written off as the customer's fault settles the question against it. The request's status is `CLOSED` at settlement; while any unit is unresolved the request shows "closed, 1 unit held", counted on the Operations page and in the reports.

**Edge cases.** Partial quantity with mixed outcomes is example 5; two settlements on one line is example 9; a quarantined unit through release, write-off, the deadline and an exception is example 10; a repair unit whose outcome is recorded before and after settlement is example 14; a successful and an unsuccessful repair after settlement, with the goodwill path and the duplicate guard, is example 15; a quarantined unit re-dispositioned to repair runs one more bounded cycle and must then be repaired, written off or sent back.

**Recommendation.** Approve the lifecycle as the owner specified it, with `heldUnits.decisionDays = 7`, `deadlineDays = 14`, `exceptionMaxDays = 30` (counted from the day-14 deadline) and `maxExceptions = 1`.
