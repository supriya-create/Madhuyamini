# Step 8.2 implementation plan: item-level refunds and settlement

Status: **plan, revision 7 (14 November 2026), awaiting the owner's explicit approval of Q8 and D8.19 and the authorisation to implement. Nothing of step 8.2 is implemented.** Revision 1 was accepted as a planning document; revision 2 (commit `71d5848`) folded in the owner's six clarifications; revision 3 (commit `277c6ac`) the four final points; revision 4 (commit `15decce`) recorded the approval of Q3 and Q7 and replaced the automatic quarantine timeout with an ADMIN resolution; revision 5 makes the owner's final documentation pass on Q8 and D8.19, as recorded in the [decision report](phase-8-step-8.2-decisions.md): the four write-off and repair outcomes distinguished with their inventory and refund consequences; the refund decision derived from the recorded disposition and fault determination, never selected, with a goodwill refund as a separate, separately audited action; the exception bounded to 30 days after the original day-14 deadline, once per unit, never extended, with mandatory resolution after it; the day-14 customer notice limited to administrative delay; and an idempotency requirement that a repair outcome recorded after settlement is paid once; revision 6 (the same day) resolves the day-58 ambiguity the owner raised against revision 5: the quarantine-to-repair re-disposition changes the unit's kind only and never its due date, deadline, exception or count, so the latest permissible end of any hold is `deadline_at + 30 days` (day 44 after inspection) on every path, and a boundary test for that date is specified (§7 criterion 10, §9); revision 7 (14 November 2026) is an engineering pass on the readiness review of revision 6 and changes no business rule, value or approval status: a line's inspection counters are final once inspected and a hold's outcome lives on its `return_held_units` row alone, moving only `held_qty` and `released_qty` (§3.1, §3.3, §3.4); the exception service requires the CHECK's lower bound, `until` strictly after `deadline_at`, with a four-point boundary test (§3.4, §7 criterion 10, §9); `held_at` comes from the injected clock with the CHECK `decision_due_at > held_at` (§3.1, §3.4); the derived keys are labels for `deriveOperationKey`, one refund row per held unit and captured payment attempt with a unique index to match (§3.1, §3.3, §4, §7 criterion 11); a goodwill refund is the unit's full value through exactly one target (§3.4); the cash-on-delivery mark-paid action carries the displayed amount and is refused if the row changed (§3.4, §7 criterion 6); the shipping charge is refunded at most once per order, with a unique index (§3.1, §3.4, §7 criterion 10); after day 44 no column of a held unit changes (§3.4, §7 criterion 10); `exceptions_count` never decreases, `exception_reason` is immutable once set and the runtime role cannot delete held-unit rows (§3.1); the 8.1 inspection service is named as it is in the code (`inspectReturn`); and the stale revision markers are corrected (§3.2, §8). The owner approved Q1, Q2, Q4, Q5 and Q6 on 3 November 2026 and Q3 and Q7 on 12 November 2026; Q8 and D8.19 are pending.

Sources: the approved [proposal](phase-8-returns-custom-artwork.md) (§3.3 R10, §4, §7.2, §8, §10.3, §11, §12, §13), the approved [decision register](phase-8-decision-register.md) (D8.5–D8.8; D8.19 pending), the [readiness document](phase-8-implementation-readiness.md) (E2, R1, R3/E5, R5, R8, R13, R15, R18) and the code landed by steps 8.0 (`93038b2`) and 8.1 (`eb1cce1`).

## 1. Scope, as approved

**In scope (proposal §13, step 8.2 "Item-level refunds and settlement: money").**

- `features/returns/refund-calculation.ts`: the pure arithmetic of §4.2–§4.3 with the deterministic per-unit rule below, and the key derivation of erratum E2.
- The settlement operation (proposal §3.3 R10): ADMIN, sensitive, under the order and request locks, from `INSPECTED` to `CLOSED`, writing `return_refund_lines` and queueing refunds through the Phase 6 engine, or recording a manual refund for cash-on-delivery orders.
- The customer-facing steps the revised decisions require before settlement: the explicit resolution choice for an exchange preference (Q1) and the settlement proposal with its dispute window (Q3).
- Tables and columns: `return_refund_lines`, `return_deductions`, `return_settlement_proposals`, `manual_refunds`, `refunds.return_request_id` and `refunds.return_held_unit_id`, the beneficiary fields D8.8 requires, the choice timestamps, the fault determination on written-off lines and the `return_held_units` table (Q8, D8.19); `users.grant_manager_since`; `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY` with its rotation job (Q5); the `bank` grant scoped to one return request with its no-self-grant CHECK, and the `returns.bank_view` and `returns.bank_grant` capabilities (Q7).
- The settlement pages, the manual-refund "mark paid" operation, the "cause" column on the refund queue, the customer's refund breakdown, the new e-mails, and reports by cause.
- The staff photograph upload the deduction rule (D8.7) depends on, using the `RETURN_INSPECTION` ledger purpose that step 8.1 created but did not use.

**Out of scope, deliberately.** Exchange settlement and replacement orders (8.3; the request form stops offering exchanges until then); lost-in-transit refunds, inspection corrections and held-stock release UI beyond what D8.19 needs (8.4); commissions (8.5–8.8); any change to the refund worker, the Razorpay adapter, checkout or inventory arithmetic.

### 3.3 Pure calculation (`features/returns/refund-calculation.ts`)

- `allocateDiscount(lines, discountInPaise)`: `share_i = floor(D × L_i / S)`; the remainder goes one paisa each to the largest fractional parts, ties by ascending variant id; shares sum exactly to `D`; `discounted_i = L_i − share_i`.
- `unitValues(discounted, q)`: `unit = floor(discounted / q)`, `r = discounted − unit × q`; units 1..(q − r) are worth `unit`, units (q − r + 1)..q are worth `unit + 1`; `value(k) = k × unit + max(0, k − (q − r))`, `value(q) = discounted`.
- `partialValue(discounted, q, alreadyRefundedUnits u, k)` = `value(u + k) − value(u)`: returned units are always valued from unit 1 upward, continuing where earlier settlements on the same line stopped (`u` = Σ `units` of earlier `ITEM_VALUE` lines for the order item). Partial returns never exceed the average per unit; sequential partials reconcile exactly to `discounted`.
- `deriveRefundDecision(disposition, faultDetermination)`: the pure policy mapping of the decision report's outcomes table (`RELEASED`, `REPAIRED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` with `SHOP_FAULT` → `REFUND_POLICY`; `WRITTEN_OFF` with any other determination and `SENT_BACK` → `NO_REFUND`); exhaustively unit-tested; the only source of `refund_decision` (Q8).
- `acceptedUnits(item, heldUnits, choices)`: from the line's inspection counters, which are final once `inspected`: restocked units + units written off at inspection with `write_off_fault = SHOP_FAULT` (policy) + `goodwill_refund_units` on a non-fault inspection write-off (the separate decision); from `return_held_units` **alone**: held units already `RESOLVED` whose derived decision is `REFUND_POLICY` or that carry a goodwill decision, and that have no `return_refund_lines` value line yet. A unit written off from a hold is never added to `write_off_qty`, so it is reached only through its held row and no unit is counted twice (a unit test resolves a held unit `WRITTEN_OFF` with `SHOP_FAULT` and asserts the count rises by exactly one). Never units still held, `return_to_customer` or `not_received`; an item with an unconfirmed exchange preference contributes nothing and blocks settlement (Q1). A held unit resolved after settlement is paid by the late-outcome path in `resolveHeldUnit` or `recordGoodwillRefund`, never by a second settlement (D8.19).
- `deductionCeiling(G_i)` = `floor(G_i × maxPercent / 100)`, where `G_i` is the line's accepted units' value after discount allocation (`partialValue` over `discounted_i`); shipping, the COD fee, other lines and the order total never enter it (Q3, the owner's clarification); `validateDeductions(line)`: each reason once, each amount ≥ 1, evidence present, condition compatible, no fault reason and no `SHOP_FAULT` determination, combined sum ≤ ceiling (Q3).
- `buildBreakdown(input)`: ITEM_VALUE per item, one DAMAGE_DEDUCTION per deduction row, SHIPPING_REFUND (`H` when the shop is at fault, that is a `SHOP_FAULT` determination or a confirmed fault reason, or when every unit of the order is accepted by policy across closed settlements and resolutions; a goodwill refund does not count), a NOTE line naming units still held, COD_FEE (0), RETURN_SHIPPING_CHARGE (only when a shop label was used and the policy amount is positive), then caps FLOOR_ZERO, CAP_PROVIDER_BALANCE, CAP_ORDER_VALUE, each adding a negative line when it bites. Output: lines and `refundTotalInPaise ≥ 0`.
- `splitAcrossAttempts(total, attempts)`: largest remaining balance first, ties by ascending attempt number; one order normally has one captured attempt, but `refundableAttempts` is an array and every path below splits over it. `deriveOperationKey(settlementKey, label)`: an RFC 4122 version 5 UUID over the request's `settlement_operation_key` and a label (erratum E2; `refunds.operation_key` and `manual_refunds.operation_key` are `uuid` columns, so a label never reaches a column). **The labels, one per refund row:** `attempt:<n>` for a settlement's refund of attempt `n`; `held:<heldUnitId>:attempt:<n>` for a late outcome; `goodwill:<heldUnitId>:attempt:<n>` and `goodwill:<returnItemId>:attempt:<n>` for a goodwill refund after settlement; `manual:held:<heldUnitId>` and `manual:goodwill:<heldUnitId>` or `manual:goodwill:<returnItemId>` for a new cash-on-delivery manual refund row. Deterministic, so a replay reproduces the same keys and `queueRefundIntent` finds its own rows; unit-tested for format, determinism and distinctness across labels and attempt numbers. Wherever this plan or the decision report writes `held:<heldUnitId>` or `goodwill:<…>`, it means these labels passed through `deriveOperationKey`.

### 3.4 Services

**Customer (`features/returns/service.ts`).**

- `confirmResolution(user, requestNumber, itemId, choice)`: `REFUND` or `WAIT`, allowed only after inspection and while the item has an exchange preference; recorded with a timestamp; reversible until settlement; event and e-mail (Q1).
- `respondToProposal(user, requestNumber, proposalNo, { accept } | { dispute, message })`: only on the request's latest proposal while it is `SENT`; an acceptance sets `ACCEPTED` with `outcome_at`; a dispute (message of at least 10 characters) sets `DISPUTED`, writes a message row and alerts the owner; both are explicit, rate-limited, request-scoped actions (Q3). Rendering the return page while a proposal is `SENT` stamps `viewed_at` once (evidence, never consent).
- `saveReturnBeneficiary(user, requestNumber, input)` for COD orders only, encrypted at once (D8.8, Q5).
- Views expose the proposal (breakdown lines and photographs), the choice and dispute state, refund lines and refund statuses per §4.5, the manual refund state; never more than the last four characters of bank details.

**Admin (`features/admin/returns/settlement.ts`, under `features/admin` so the signature scan covers it).**

- `proposeSettlement(ctx, id, input, now)`: `returns.settle` (sensitive); validates decisions (every item once; deductions per Q3; written-off choices per Q8; shop label flag); computes the breakdown under the lock; inserts the next `return_settlement_proposals` row (`SENT`) and the outbox row `return.settlement_proposed` (dedupe key per proposal number) in the same transaction, marking an open previous proposal `SUPERSEDED`; a revision after a dispute is the same operation and counts a round, refused beyond `maxDisputeRounds` unless every deduction drops to zero; audited `return.settlement_proposed`. Required whenever any deduction is present; optional otherwise.
- `resendProposal(ctx, id, proposalNo)`: `returns.settle`; only for an `UNDELIVERED` proposal; queues a fresh outbox row for the same proposal (the window will run from its delivery); audited `return.settlement_proposal_resent`.
- `decideSettlement(ctx, id, proposalNo, { deductions, reason })`: `returns.settle` (sensitive); allowed only when the latest proposal is `DISPUTED` and an earlier proposal of the request was disputed too (rounds exhausted); amounts at or below the latest proposal; reason of at least 20 characters; sets `DECIDED` with `decided_by_id`; e-mails `return.settlement_decided`; audited `return.settlement_decided`.
- `settleReturn(ctx, id, input, now)`: `enforceActorLimit(settlePerActor)`; `runAudited` (`return.settled`, `returns.settle`); transaction: `lockOrder` → `lockReturnRequest` → re-read; replay by `settlement_operation_key` (same key returns the stored summary; another key on a settled request is refused); status guard `INSPECTED`; **preconditions**: no item with an unconfirmed exchange preference (Q1); the latest proposal is `ACCEPTED`, `DECIDED`, or `LAPSED` with `delivered_at` set and its outbox row re-read as `SENT`, or has no deductions (Q3); the submitted decisions equal that proposal's snapshot (otherwise re-propose); recompute the breakdown under the lock with `refundableAttempts`; write deductions (`return_deductions` rows and the line totals) and `return_refund_lines` with the order id (value lines for held units carry `return_held_unit_id`; goodwill units are counted only where a goodwill decision is already recorded; the `SHIPPING_REFUND` line only when no request on the order has one yet, the unique per-order index being the backstop); **online**: `splitAcrossAttempts` over `refundableAttempts`, then `queueRefundIntent` per attempt with `reason: "customer_return"`, `operationKey: deriveOperationKey(key, "attempt:<n>")` and `returnRequestId` (no `returnHeldUnitId`: one settlement row can pay several units); **COD**: `manual_refunds` row `PENDING` with the request's beneficiary (refused `BENEFICIARY_MISSING`); held units contribute nothing unless already `RESOLVED` with a refund decision, and a NOTE line names those still held (their `return_held_units` rows exist since inspection); `moveTo(CLOSED)` with `settled_at`, `closed_at`, `settlement_operation_key`; return event, order events per refund, outbox `return.closed`; audit `after` with totals, the proposal number and the **basis** (`ACCEPTED`, `LAPSED`, `DECIDED`, `NO_DEDUCTIONS`) only.
- `inspectReturn` (8.1, extended; earlier revisions of this plan called it `completeInspection`, which is the Phase 7 order-parcel inspection in `features/admin/orders/service.ts`, a different function): a written-off line requires `write_off_fault`, prefilled from the request's reason and confirmed by the inspector; every `REPAIR` and `QUARANTINE` unit gets a `return_held_units` row in the inspection transaction with `held_at = now`, `decision_due_at = now + decisionDays` and `deadline_at = now + deadlineDays`, all three from the `now` the action injects (the same instant as `inspected_at`; never `DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP`, so the CHECKs `decision_due_at > held_at` and `deadline_at > decision_due_at` compare values from one clock, the lesson of the access-token regression fixed on 12 November 2026; the integration test pins `now` away from the wall clock exactly as that regression test does); `held_qty = repair_qty + quarantine_qty` and `released_qty = 0` as today; the line's inspection counters, `condition` and `write_off_fault` are final from this commit (trigger, §3.1); return event `UNIT_HELD` per unit.
- `resolveHeldUnit(ctx, heldUnitId, { disposition, faultDetermination?, reason })` (`returns.settle`, sensitive; `runAudited` `return.held_resolved`; conditional on `state IN (HELD, OVERDUE, RESOLUTION_REQUIRED, EXCEPTION)`; under `orders → return_requests` locks): takes **no refund field**; requires `faultDetermination` for `WRITTEN_OFF` and refuses it for any other disposition; computes `refund_decision` with `deriveRefundDecision` and writes disposition, fault determination and the derived decision together (the CHECKs refuse any other combination, also under direct SQL); `RELEASED` / `REPAIRED` → `applyAdjustment` `RESTOCK_ON_RETURN` +1 and, on the line, `held_qty −1`, `released_qty +1`; `WRITTEN_OFF` → no stock movement (the unit never entered stock, proposal §9.2) and `held_qty −1` only: the write-off lives on the held row (`disposition`, `fault_determination`), and `write_off_qty`, `repair_qty` and `quarantine_qty` stay as the inspector left them (the counter trigger of §3.1 refuses otherwise, and the 8.1 CHECK `return_items_inspection_sums` stays true); `SENT_BACK` → an outbound `return_shipments` row and `held_qty −1`. **Late-outcome payment, once:** if the request is not yet settled, nothing is queued and the next settlement pays the unit (its `ITEM_VALUE` line carries `return_held_unit_id`); if the request is already settled and the derived decision is `REFUND_POLICY`, the unit's value (plus the shipping line when D8.6's condition becomes true and no request on the order has refunded it yet, the unique per-order index being the backstop) is split with `splitAcrossAttempts` over `refundableAttempts` and queued through `queueRefundIntent` per attempt under `deriveOperationKey(settlementKey, "held:<heldUnitId>:attempt:<n>")` with `returnHeldUnitId` set, for an online order (one refund row per held unit and attempt; the unique index on `refunds (return_held_unit_id, payment_id)` refuses a second); or, for a COD order, added to the still-`PENDING` manual refund by a conditional update (`WHERE status = 'PENDING'`: `amount_in_paise` plus the value, `amended_at = now`; the value line carries `manual_refund_id` and the unit, so every amendment is traceable), or written as a new `PENDING` manual refund row under `deriveOperationKey(settlementKey, "manual:held:<heldUnitId>")` when the earlier one was already paid or cancelled; `manual_refund_id` is stored on the held unit and the refund rows carry `return_held_unit_id`. A repeated call finds the unit `RESOLVED` and returns the stored result; the unique `refunds.operation_key`, the unique refund index per unit and attempt, the unique value-line index and the conditional updates are the backstops. E-mail `return.held_resolved`.
- `recordGoodwillRefund(ctx, target, { units?, reason })` (`returns.settle`, sensitive; its own audit event `return.goodwill_refund`; `target` a held unit for a unit written off from a hold, or a written-off line for units written off at inspection; a unit is reachable through exactly one of the two, since a hold write-off is never added to `write_off_qty`): allowed only when the policy decision is `NO_REFUND` on a `WRITTEN_OFF` with `CUSTOMER_FAULT` or `NO_FAULT`, once per target, `units` between 1 and `write_off_qty` for a line (absent for a held unit), reason ≥ 20 characters; **the amount is never an input**: it is the full value of the unit, or of the named units, under §3.3 (`partialValue` continuing from the line's earlier settlements), with no deduction; records the goodwill columns beside the unchanged policy decision; before settlement the units are counted by `acceptedUnits`; after settlement the refund is queued by the same late-outcome rules, per attempt under the `goodwill:` labels, with `returnHeldUnitId` set for a held unit (so the unique index per unit and attempt also refuses a goodwill row beside a policy row), or added to the pending manual refund (`amended_at`) or a new one under `manual:goodwill:<…>` for a COD order; return event and e-mail `return.goodwill_refund`. Refused, with a REFUSED audit row, on a `SHOP_FAULT` unit, a send-back, a unit not yet resolved, a line target naming more units than `write_off_qty`, or a second time.
- `redispositionToRepair(ctx, heldUnitId)` (quarantine only, once, from any non-resolved state; `returns.settle`, sensitive, audited `return.held_redispositioned`): sets `kind = REPAIR` and `redispositioned_at` and **changes nothing else**: `state`, `held_at`, `decision_due_at`, `deadline_at`, `exception_until` and `exceptions_count` are untouched (the date columns are immutable by trigger), so the repair runs under the unit's original clock, the exception ceiling is unchanged and no re-disposition can extend a hold; a second re-disposition is refused.
- `recordHeldUnitException(ctx, heldUnitId, { reason, until })` (`returns.settle`, sensitive, audited `return.held_exception`): from `OVERDUE` or `RESOLUTION_REQUIRED` only; reason ≥ 20 characters; `until` **strictly after `deadline_at`** (the same lower bound as the CHECK: an exception granted from `OVERDUE`, between day 7 and day 14, still ends after the day-14 deadline, so the job can never return the unit to `RESOLUTION_REQUIRED` before its own deadline), strictly after now (service only; the CHECK cannot see the clock), and **at most `deadline_at + exceptionMaxDays`** (counted from the original day-14 deadline, whatever day it is granted and whether or not the unit was re-dispositioned). **Boundary, by the service and by the CHECK:** `until = deadline_at` is refused (before), `deadline_at + 1 second` is accepted, `deadline_at + exceptionMaxDays` exactly is accepted (at), and one second later is refused (after); refused when `exceptions_count ≥ maxExceptions` (once per unit); sets `EXCEPTION`, `exception_until` (immutable from then on; there is no extend operation) and `exceptions_count = 1`.
- The returns clock job moves `HELD → OVERDUE` at `decision_due_at` (daily owner alert, deduplicated per unit and day), `OVERDUE → RESOLUTION_REQUIRED` at `deadline_at` (escalated daily alert, system audit row `return.held_resolution_required`, one customer e-mail `return.held_delayed` whose template carries no amount and no word of acceptance, approval, refund or deduction), sends one reminder the day before `exception_until`, and moves `EXCEPTION → RESOLUTION_REQUIRED` when it passes (system audit row `return.held_exception_expired`), after which resolution is mandatory. After `deadline_at + exceptionMaxDays` (day 44) the job changes no column of any held unit: its only remaining writes for the unit are the escalated daily `ops.alert` rows, one per unit and day, until a person resolves it. **The job never resolves a unit, never moves stock and never queues money** (D8.19). Deviation from the proposal, stated: §9.2 let STAFF release held stock under `inventory.adjust`; because the owner's rule records the fault determination with the disposition and derives the refund from them, the release is now part of the ADMIN resolution and STAFF see the held-unit cards read-only.

- `markManualRefundPaid(ctx, manualRefundId, { reference, note, amountShownInPaise })`, `cancelManualRefund` (`returns.manual_refund`, sensitive) as revision 1, with **optimistic concurrency on the amount**: the form carries the amount it displayed, and the update is conditional on `status = 'PENDING' AND amount_in_paise = amountShownInPaise`; a late outcome that amended the row in between makes the update match no row, the action is refused `AMOUNT_CHANGED` with a REFUSED audit row, and the page re-renders the new amount with its `amended_at`, so the owner pays what the row now says or nothing. The amount paid is the row's, re-read in the transaction, never the form's. Cancellation is conditional on `PENDING` alone.
- `issueBankGrant(ctx, input)` / `revokeBankGrant(ctx, grantId, cause)` (`features/admin/customers/service.ts`): `returns.bank_grant` (sensitive), which `requirePermission` resolves as the ADMIN role or `users.grant_manager_since IS NOT NULL`, read live with the context; the grant names one return request, one grantee (an enrolled, verified staff account other than the issuer), a reason and an expiry of at most 24 hours (default `bankGrantDefaultHours`); self-grant, an unenrolled grantee, an over-long expiry or a missing reason are refused with a REFUSED row `grant.bank.refused`; `enforceActorLimit(grantPerActor)`; revocation by the issuer, any grant manager or ADMIN is immediate; both write `grant.bank.issued` / `grant.bank.revoked` and an owner notice. A role change or `admin:grant-manager --remove` revokes the bank grants the person holds and issued. The daily sweep writes `grant.bank.expired` once per lapsed grant with the reveals it served. Script `npm run admin:grant-manager -- <email> [--remove]` sets or clears the designation (verified, enrolled account only; `script:` audit row; refuses production without confirmation, like `admin:promote`).
- `revealBeneficiary(ctx, manualRefundId, reason)`: requires an active `bank` grant for `ctx.user` on this return request (re-read `FOR SHARE`), a fresh second factor, the explicit reveal action; writes a `staff_pii_reveals` row (`fields = ['bank']`, ten minutes) and `return.bank.revealed`; counts against `revealPerActor` (Q7). STAFF without a grant and ADMIN without a grant both get a refusal with a REFUSED audit row `return.bank.reveal_refused`.

**Keys (`server/security/customer-data.ts`, job `rotate-customer-data-key`).** `encryptCustomerSecret`/`decryptCustomerSecret` with `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY` (version n) and `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY_PREVIOUS` (n − 1); the job re-encrypts rows with `beneficiary_key_version < n` in batches of 100 with a conditional update per row, is idempotent and resumable, reports counts, writes one system audit row per run, and lists undecryptable rows by id without changing them (Q5). ## 4. Transactions, idempotency, concurrency, audit, recovery

- **One transaction** per settlement under `orders → return_requests` locks with refund reads; no inventory (stock moved at inspection) and no second order (replacement orders are 8.3). Proposals, choices and disputes are their own short transactions under the same locks.
- **Idempotency**: settlement key on the request (unique); per-attempt refund keys as version-5 UUIDs derived from it (E2); replay returns the stored summary; another key on a settled request is refused; `queueRefundIntent` and `refunds.operation_key` are the backstops. A held unit is paid at most once, by exactly one path chosen under the locks at resolution: the next settlement (not yet settled) or refunds queued at the resolution under keys derived from the labels `held:<heldUnitId>:attempt:<n>` (already settled), a goodwill refund under `goodwill:<heldUnitId>:attempt:<n>`, one row per captured attempt the amount is split across; the conditional update from an unresolved state, the unique `refunds.operation_key`, the unique index on `refunds (return_held_unit_id, payment_id)`, the unique value-line index on `return_held_unit_id`, the conditional manual-refund amendment and the mark-paid amount guard make a repeated outcome, a retried request or a resolution racing a settlement pay once, one refund row per unit and attempt (D8.19). The shipping charge is refunded at most once per order: the service checks under the order lock and the unique per-order index on `SHIPPING_REFUND` lines is the backstop.
- **Preconditions are re-checked under the lock**: no unconfirmed exchange item; proposal acceptance or lapsed window with no open dispute; decisions equal to the snapshot; caps from `refundableAttempts`.
- **Concurrency**: two settlements of one request serialise on the request lock and the second fails the status guard; a settlement racing a goodwill refund serialises on the order lock; a customer dispute racing a settlement is resolved by the lock (whichever commits first wins; a settlement that commits first already had the window lapsed, so the late dispute is recorded as a post-settlement message and alerts the owner); a late outcome racing the owner's mark-paid form on a cash-on-delivery manual refund serialises on the request lock and the displayed amount decides: an amendment that commits first makes the mark-paid refuse and re-render, a mark-paid that commits first leaves the late outcome to open a new `PENDING` row.
- **Audit**: `return.settlement_proposed`, `return.settlement_proposal_resent`, `return.settlement_proposal_undelivered` and `return.settlement_proposal_lapsed` (system actor), `return.settlement_decided`, `return.settled` (totals, proposal number and basis), `return.held_resolved`, `return.goodwill_refund`, `return.held_exception`, `return.held_resolution_required` and `return.held_exception_expired` (system actor), `manual_refund` rows for payment and cancellation, `grant.bank.issued` / `refused` / `revoked` / `expired`, `return.bank.revealed` / `reveal_refused`; customer actions (accept, dispute, choice) are return events and message rows; `return_refund_lines`, `return_deductions` and `return_settlement_proposals` immutable or append-only; allow-lists exclude free text and beneficiary data.
- **Failure recovery**: provider failures stay with the worker (retries, `FAILED` with alert, `retryRefund`, external marking); unpaid manual refunds alert daily from day 3 and escalate at day 10; a crash after commit is a replay on resubmission; the rotation job is resumable.

## 7. Acceptance criteria (measurable)

1. A settlement of an online order queues refunds whose sum equals the breakdown total, never exceeds the captured balance under any combination in the test matrix, and whose rows carry `return_request_id`, reason `customer_return` and version-5 keys derived from the settlement key.
2. The same settlement submitted twice with the same key produces one set of refund rows and lines; another key on a settled request is refused; concurrent settlements produce one result.
3. No item with an unconfirmed exchange preference can be settled; the customer's choice (refund or wait) is recorded, reversible until settlement, reminded at 3, 7 and 14 days, and the request is marked on hold after 30 days with no automatic conversion.
4. Every deduction has a reason from the list, at least one staff photograph from the request, a compatible condition, is never on a fault line, and the combined deductions of a line never exceed 30 % of the accepted units' value after discount allocation (a fixture with a coupon and a shipping charge, example 13, refuses a deduction that fits only under the undiscounted line, the line plus shipping or the order total); a settlement with deductions requires the latest proposal to be accepted, lapsed after confirmed delivery (a test forces the outbox row to `FAILED` and proves the proposal becomes `UNDELIVERED` and settlement stays refused however much time passes), or decided after two dispute rounds; the settlement audit row names the basis and the proposal number.
5. Per-unit values follow the deterministic rule: for every `q` ≤ 10, every `r` and every sequential split of partial returns, the partial values are non-decreasing in steps of `unit` or `unit + 1` and reconcile exactly to the discounted line value; Appendix A and the decision report's examples 1–14 pass as fixtures.
6. A COD settlement creates one pending manual refund with encrypted details and no `refunds` row; a `bank` grant can be issued only by ADMIN or a grant manager, never to themselves (refused by the service and by the CHECK under direct SQL), only for one request and at most 24 hours; reveal requires that grant, a fresh second factor and an explicit action, and writes an audit row; revocation masks on the next render; expiry writes an audit row; no surface other than an audited reveal carries more than the last four characters (a seeded account number is absent from list and report DTOs, audit rows, outbox payloads, every e-mail template and the server log of the COD path); mark-paid is conditional on `PENDING` and on the amount the form displayed (an amount amended by a late outcome in between is refused with a REFUSED audit row and re-rendered, and the row is then paid once at its new amount; a stale amount submitted twice pays nothing), audited and e-mailed; unpaid rows alert daily from day 3 and escalate at day 10; ciphertext is nulled after retention; the rotation job re-encrypts every row and reports zero candidates afterwards.
7. The worker processes settlement refunds end to end through the fake gateway, including a lost response and a non-retryable refusal; a failed refund leaves the request closed and the customer page states the delay.
8. The customer page shows the proposal, the breakdown and each refund's state; the admin queue shows the cause; reports show refunds by cause, manual refunds and held units, from real rows only.
9. Every audit row for proposal, settlement, release, payment, cancellation, grant and reveal exists with the right outcome and no beneficiary data.
10. No held unit is ever resolved by the system: the job marks `OVERDUE` at the due date with a daily alert, `RESOLUTION_REQUIRED` at the deadline with an escalated daily alert and the administrative-delay e-mail (which carries no amount and no word of acceptance, approval, refund or deduction), returns an expired exception to `RESOLUTION_REQUIRED`, and changes nothing on a repeated run; `held_at`, `decision_due_at` and `deadline_at` are written from the injected `now` (a test pins `now` away from the wall clock and the CHECKs `decision_due_at > held_at` and `deadline_at > decision_due_at` hold); a resolution records disposition and fault determination, the refund decision is derived and matches the outcomes table in every case (successful repair, unsuccessful repair, shop-fault write-off, non-fault write-off, release, send-back), no form input can set it, and the CHECKs refuse a disagreeing row under direct SQL; a resolution moves only `held_qty` and `released_qty` on the line (a hold write-off leaves `write_off_qty` unchanged and the 8.1 CHECK `return_items_inspection_sums` true; an `UPDATE` of any inspection counter, `condition` or `write_off_fault` on an inspected line is refused by trigger) and `acceptedUnits` counts the unit exactly once, through its held row; a goodwill refund is a separate audited action, allowed once and only beside a `NO_REFUND` decision on a non-fault write-off, pays the unit's full value, and reaches a unit through exactly one target (held row or line); an exception is refused before day 7, when it would end at or before `deadline_at`, when it would end more than 30 days after the day-14 deadline (**boundary**, by the service and by the CHECK: `deadline_at` refused, `deadline_at + 1 second` accepted, exactly `deadline_at + 30 days`, day 44 after inspection, accepted, one second later refused), and a second time, and cannot be extended (`exception_until` and `exception_reason` immutable once set, `exceptions_count` never decreasing, by trigger); a quarantine-to-repair re-disposition changes the unit's kind only, so its state, due date, deadline, exception and count are unchanged and the same ceiling applies afterwards; no transition resets a unit's dates, and the clock job run day by day over a unit that takes every transition changes no column of the unit after day 44 while its escalated daily alert rows continue; no `DELETE` on `return_held_units` succeeds for the runtime role; the shipping refund is produced at the first settlement or resolution where D8.6's condition is decidable and **at most once per order** (two held units of one order resolved separately produce one `SHIPPING_REFUND` line, and a second for the same order is refused by the unique index under direct SQL).
11. A held unit is paid at most once: a repair or quarantine outcome recorded before settlement is paid by that settlement and the resolution queues nothing; one recorded after settlement is paid at the outcome under keys derived from `held:<heldUnitId>:attempt:<n>`, which are valid version-5 UUIDs in `refunds.operation_key`; recording the outcome twice, retrying after a crash, replaying the job, racing a resolution against a settlement, and a goodwill decision after a policy refund all produce exactly **one refund row per held unit and captured payment attempt** (a two-attempt fixture with a balance on each produces one row per attempt and no more on any replay, and a second row for the same unit and attempt is refused by the unique index on `refunds (return_held_unit_id, payment_id)` under direct SQL), or one manual-refund amount per unit, for online and COD orders.
12. The complete gate passes locally and in CI; the Phase 6 and 8.1 suites run unchanged; migration and rollback rehearsed on a scratch database in the order 8.2 then 8.1.

## 10. Deployment, migration, rollback and operations

- **Secrets**: `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY` provisioned before deploy; `_PREVIOUS` only during rotation; retired keys sealed for the backup retention window (Phase 11) and then destroyed with a recorded date.
- **Migration**: additive except the replaced grant CHECK; rehearse apply, rollback and re-apply on a scratch database; rollback order 8.2 before 8.1.
- **Runbooks to add**: proposing and settling a return; an undelivered proposal (contact, resend); handling a dispute and recording a decision; designating a grant manager; paying a manual refund with a `bank` grant issued by someone else; rotating the key; what the sweep deletes; an on-hold exchange item; an overdue held unit, a unit past its deadline, recording the one exception, recording a goodwill refund.
- **Monitoring**: existing failed-refund alerts; new alerts for undelivered proposals, unanswered choices, open disputes past the response clock, unpaid transfers, overdue holds, units past their deadline (escalated) and expiring exceptions; the Operations page shows every open obligation with its deadline.
- **Operational risks**: staff proposing deductions without photographs (refused); owners forgetting transfers (alerts); a settlement on a COD order without bank details (refused with a message); key loss (unrecoverable details; the transfer is made by contacting the customer).
