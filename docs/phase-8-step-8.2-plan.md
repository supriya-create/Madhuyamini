# Step 8.2 implementation plan: item-level refunds and settlement

Status: **plan awaiting the owner's approval (3 November 2026). Nothing of step 8.2 is implemented.** This plan is derived from the approved [proposal](phase-8-returns-custom-artwork.md) (§3.3 R10, §4, §7.2, §8, §10.3, §11, §12, §13), the approved [decision register](phase-8-decision-register.md) (D8.5–D8.8), the [readiness document](phase-8-implementation-readiness.md) (E2, R1, R3/E5, R5, R8, R13, R15, R18) and the code landed by steps 8.0 (`93038b2`) and 8.1 (`eb1cce1`). It does not widen the step: anything not listed in §13's 8.2 row is named below only to say that it stays out.

## 1. Scope, as approved

**In scope (proposal §13, step 8.2 "Item-level refunds and settlement: money").**

- `features/returns/refund-calculation.ts`: the pure arithmetic of §4.2–§4.3 (discount allocation, unit values, breakdown lines, caps, attempt split) and the key derivation of erratum E2.
- The settlement operation (proposal §3.3 R10): ADMIN, sensitive, under the order and request locks, from `INSPECTED` to `CLOSED`, writing `return_refund_lines` and queueing refunds through the Phase 6 engine, or recording a manual refund for cash-on-delivery orders.
- Tables and columns: `return_refund_lines`, `manual_refunds`, `refunds.return_request_id`; the beneficiary fields D8.8 requires; `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY` for encrypting them.
- The settlement page, the manual-refund "mark paid" operation, the "cause" column on the refund queue, the customer's refund breakdown, the `return.closed` e-mail, and the reports rows for refunds by cause.
- The staff photograph upload the deduction rule (D8.7) depends on, using the `RETURN_INSPECTION` ledger purpose that step 8.1 created but did not use.

**Out of scope, deliberately.** Exchange settlement and replacement orders (8.3); lost-in-transit refunds, inspection corrections and held-stock release (8.4); commissions (8.5–8.8); any change to the refund worker, the Razorpay adapter, checkout or inventory arithmetic. One consequence needs a decision (§8, Q1): a return item whose preference is "exchange" reaches settlement in 8.2 before replacement orders exist.

### 3.3 Pure calculation (`features/returns/refund-calculation.ts`, unit-tested against Appendix A)

- `allocateDiscount(lines, discountInPaise)`: largest-remainder allocation in ascending variant id; the sum equals `subtotal − discount` exactly.
- `unitValues(line)`: `unit` and the count of units carrying one extra paisa (the last ones), so `k` units are worth `k × unit + max(0, k − (q − remainder))`.
- `acceptedUnits(item, policy, refundWrittenOff)`: restock + repair + quarantine, plus write-off when the reason is a fault reason or `refundWrittenOff` is set; never `return_to_customer` or `not_received`.
- `buildBreakdown(input)`: ITEM_VALUE per item, DAMAGE_DEDUCTION (bounded by `min(G, floor(G × max% / 100))`, refused on fault lines, refused without evidence ids), SHIPPING_REFUND (`H` when the shop is at fault or every unit of the order is accepted across closed settlements, else 0), COD_FEE (0), RETURN_SHIPPING_CHARGE (negative, only when staff mark a shop label used and the policy amount is positive), then caps in order: FLOOR_ZERO, CAP_PROVIDER_BALANCE (`Σ captured − refunded` from `refundableAttempts`), CAP_ORDER_VALUE (`total − already refunded − exchange credit consumed`, the last term supplied by the caller and zero until 8.3). Each cap that bites adds a negative line. Output: lines and `refundTotalInPaise ≥ 0`.
- `splitAcrossAttempts(total, attempts)`: largest remaining balance first; one order normally has one captured attempt.
- `deriveOperationKey(settlementKey, label)`: RFC 4122 version 5 UUID over the settlement key and a label such as `attempt:1` (erratum E2), so a replay reproduces the same `refunds.operation_key` values.
- `manualRefundAmount` is the same breakdown; the COD branch differs only in where the money goes.

**`features/admin/returns/settlement.ts`** (under `features/admin` so the signature scan covers it):

## 4. Transactions, idempotency, concurrency, audit, recovery

- **One transaction** per settlement (§5.5 lock order: `orders → return_requests → payments/refunds` reads; no inventory, since stock moved at inspection; no second order, since replacement orders are 8.3). The provider is never called inside it; the `refund-payments` worker calls it later from the intent rows.
- **Idempotency**: the settlement key is rendered into the form and stored on `return_requests.settlement_operation_key` (unique). A replay with the same key returns the stored summary without recomputation. Per-attempt refund keys are version-5 UUIDs derived from the settlement key (E2), so even a crash between `queueRefundIntent` calls cannot produce duplicates on a retry: `queueRefundIntent` is itself idempotent on `reference`, and `refunds.operation_key` is unique. A different key on an already settled request is refused (`SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_DONE`), never silently re-settled.
- **Concurrency**: two settlements of one request serialise on the request lock and the second fails the `INSPECTED` guard; a settlement racing an admin goodwill refund serialises on the order lock and the later one sees the earlier rows through `refundableAttempts`; two settlements on different requests of one order (sequential returns) sequence their references and share the caps. Manual-refund payment is a conditional update on `PENDING`.
- **Caps are computed under the lock from the database**, never from the page: cap 2 from `refundableAttempts`, cap 3 from the order total minus every non-failed refund of the order.
- **Audit**: `return.settled` SUCCESS with totals (`refundTotal`, `linesRefunded`, `deductions`, `shippingRefund`, `refundCount`, `manual`), REFUSED on a stale second factor or limit, FAILED on a rule refusal inside the work; `return_refund_lines` immutable; `manual_refund` target rows for payment, cancellation and reveal; allow-lists exclude free text, ciphertext and last four characters.
- **Failure recovery**: provider failures stay with the worker (`FAILED` after the backoff schedule, owner alert, `retryRefund` with the balance re-check, `markRefundedExternally` verified with `listRefunds`); the request remains `CLOSED` and the customer page shows "refund delayed" until `PROCESSED`. A manual refund never pays itself: an unpaid row older than `cod.pendingAlertDays` raises a daily owner alert. A crash after the transaction committed but before the response reached the browser is a replay (same key) on resubmission.
- **Rounding**: integer paise throughout; the largest-remainder allocation is deterministic and total-preserving; Appendix A of the proposal is the unit-test vector set.

## 5. Payment-provider implications

- **Razorpay (online orders)**: settlements become ordinary refund intents executed by the existing worker through the existing adapter: `receipt` = our reference (idempotent at the provider; references stay under 40 characters), `listRefunds` before `createRefund` recovers lost responses, partial refunds are allowed and their sum is bounded by the capture both by our caps and by the provider, `refund.processed` webhooks and polling move rows to `PROCESSED`, and the attempt becomes `REFUNDED` only when processed refunds reach the capture. Nothing in the adapter or worker changes; the fake gateway covers tests, including a lost response and a non-retryable refusal.
- **Cash on delivery**: no provider; the collected amount is the COD attempt's `amount_in_paise` and the refund is a bank transfer the owner makes outside the system and records with its reference. The COD attempt never becomes `REFUNDED`; reports show manual refunds separately. Bank details are collected per D8.8, encrypted at rest, shown as four characters, revealed only to ADMIN with a fresh second factor and an audit row, and deleted after retention.
- **Edge**: a delivered order without a captured attempt cannot exist through the normal flows; if data drift ever produces one, cap 2 yields zero and the settlement closes with an explanatory line rather than failing. Tested.
- **Not relevant here but carried**: R5 (late capture of a lapsed replacement) and E5/R3 (hook lock order) concern replacement orders and arrive with 8.3; 8.2 creates no order rows and takes no second order lock.

## 7. Acceptance criteria (measurable)

1. A settlement of an online order queues refunds whose sum equals the breakdown total, never exceeds the captured balance under any combination in the test matrix, and whose rows carry `return_request_id`, reason `customer_return` and version-5 operation keys derived from the settlement key.
2. The same settlement submitted twice with the same key produces exactly one set of refund rows and lines; a different key on a settled request is refused; two concurrent settlements produce one result.
3. A settlement and a concurrent goodwill refund on the same order never exceed the capture; a second return on the same order sequences references and respects the caps.
4. Deductions above the ceiling, without a reason, without evidence, or on fault lines are refused by the service and (where expressible) by the database; shipping is refunded only under the approved rule; the COD fee is never refunded.
5. A COD settlement creates one `PENDING` manual refund with encrypted beneficiary details and no `refunds` row; "mark paid" is conditional, audited and e-mailed; unpaid rows alert after the configured days; beneficiary ciphertext is nulled by the sweep after retention.
6. The `refund-payments` worker processes settlement refunds end to end through the fake gateway, including a lost response and a non-retryable refusal; a failed refund leaves the request `CLOSED` and the customer page states the delay.
7. The customer page shows the breakdown and each refund's state as in §4.5; the admin refund queue shows the cause; reports show refunds by cause and manual refunds, from real rows only.
8. Every audit row for settlement, payment, cancellation and reveal exists with the right outcome and carries no beneficiary data; the redaction test proves it.

## 10. Deployment, migration, rollback and operations

- **Secrets before deploy**: `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY` (32 random bytes, base64url) must exist in production before the step is deployed, or COD settlements refuse; `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY_PREVIOUS` only during rotation. CI needs no real key (the test setup uses a fixed one).
- **Migration**: additive; applied by `prisma migrate deploy` in CI's fresh database and by hand on the development database; the runtime role gets the new tables through default privileges and loses UPDATE/DELETE on `return_refund_lines` explicitly. Rehearse apply, rollback and re-apply on a scratch database, in the order 8.2 then 8.1 for rollback.
- **Rollback**: code rollback alone is safe (additive columns, no reads of the new tables elsewhere); schema rollback refuses while settled requests exist unless refund rows are detached first, which is the intended protection for financial records.
- **Exposure**: nothing customer-facing changes until an ADMIN settles a return; the bank-details fields appear only on COD returns. Settlement is ADMIN-only with a fresh second factor and a per-actor limit.
- **Runbooks to add** (`docs/admin.md` §13 style): settling a return and reading the breakdown; paying a manual refund and recording the reference; handling a `FAILED` refund after settlement (retry or external mark); rotating `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY`; what the retention sweep deletes.
- **Monitoring**: existing ops alerts for failed refunds and the new daily alert for unpaid manual refunds; the Operations page shows the refund queue and manual refunds pending.
- **Operational risks**: staff entering deductions without photographs (refused, so trained in the runbook); owners forgetting a transfer (alert); a settlement on a COD order before the customer gave details (refused with a message, and the customer page asks for them); key loss (bank details unrecoverable; the request can still close and the transfer be made by contacting the customer).
