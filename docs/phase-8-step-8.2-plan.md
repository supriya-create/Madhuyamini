**Out of scope, deliberately.** Exchange settlement and replacement orders (8.3; the request form stops offering exchanges until then); lost-in-transit refunds, inspection corrections and held-stock release UI beyond what D8.19 needs (8.4); commissions (8.5–8.8); any change to the refund worker, the Razorpay adapter, checkout or inventory arithmetic.

### 3.3 Pure calculation (`features/returns/refund-calculation.ts`)

- `allocateDiscount(lines, discountInPaise)`: `share_i = floor(D × L_i / S)`; the remainder goes one paisa each to the largest fractional parts, ties by ascending variant id; shares sum exactly to `D`; `discounted_i = L_i − share_i`.
- `unitValues(discounted, q)`: `unit = floor(discounted / q)`, `r = discounted − unit × q`; units 1..(q − r) are worth `unit`, units (q − r + 1)..q are worth `unit + 1`; `value(k) = k × unit + max(0, k − (q − r))`, `value(q) = discounted`.
- `partialValue(discounted, q, alreadyRefundedUnits u, k)` = `value(u + k) − value(u)`: returned units are always valued from unit 1 upward, continuing where earlier settlements on the same line stopped (`u` = Σ `units` of earlier `ITEM_VALUE` lines for the order item). Partial returns never exceed the average per unit; sequential partials reconcile exactly to `discounted`.
- `acceptedUnits(item, policy, choices)`: restock + repair (+ quarantine when `heldUnits.refundQuarantineAtSettlement`, else deferred per D8.19) + write-off when the reason is a fault reason or `refundWrittenOff` was chosen; never `return_to_customer` or `not_received`; an item with an unconfirmed exchange preference contributes nothing and blocks settlement (Q1).
- `deductionCeiling(G)` = `floor(G × maxPercent / 100)`; `validateDeductions(line)`: each reason once, each amount ≥ 1, evidence present, condition compatible, no fault reason, combined sum ≤ ceiling (Q3).
- `buildBreakdown(input)`: ITEM_VALUE per item, one DAMAGE_DEDUCTION per deduction row, SHIPPING_REFUND (`H` when the shop is at fault or every unit of the order is accepted across closed settlements), COD_FEE (0), RETURN_SHIPPING_CHARGE (only when a shop label was used and the policy amount is positive), then caps FLOOR_ZERO, CAP_PROVIDER_BALANCE, CAP_ORDER_VALUE, each adding a negative line when it bites. Output: lines and `refundTotalInPaise ≥ 0`.
- `splitAcrossAttempts(total, attempts)` and `deriveOperationKey(settlementKey, label)` (RFC 4122 version 5, erratum E2) as in revision 1.

## 4. Transactions, idempotency, concurrency, audit, recovery

- **One transaction** per settlement under `orders → return_requests` locks with refund reads; no inventory (stock moved at inspection) and no second order (replacement orders are 8.3). Proposals, choices and disputes are their own short transactions under the same locks.
- **Idempotency**: settlement key on the request (unique); per-attempt refund keys as version-5 UUIDs derived from it (E2); replay returns the stored summary; another key on a settled request is refused; `queueRefundIntent` and `refunds.operation_key` are the backstops. A held-unit release uses a derived key `held:<itemId>` so a repeated release cannot refund twice.
- **Preconditions are re-checked under the lock**: no unconfirmed exchange item; proposal acceptance or lapsed window with no open dispute; decisions equal to the snapshot; caps from `refundableAttempts`.
- **Concurrency**: two settlements of one request serialise on the request lock and the second fails the status guard; a settlement racing a goodwill refund serialises on the order lock; a customer dispute racing a settlement is resolved by the lock (whichever commits first wins; a settlement that commits first already had the window lapsed, so the late dispute is recorded as a post-settlement message and alerts the owner).
- **Audit**: `return.settlement_proposed`, `return.settled` (totals), `return.held_released`, `manual_refund` rows for payment, cancellation and reveal, grant issue and revocation; `return_refund_lines` and `return_deductions` immutable; allow-lists exclude free text and beneficiary data.
- **Failure recovery**: provider failures stay with the worker (retries, `FAILED` with alert, `retryRefund`, external marking); unpaid manual refunds alert daily from day 3 and escalate at day 10; a crash after commit is a replay on resubmission; the rotation job is resumable.

## 7. Acceptance criteria (measurable)

1. A settlement of an online order queues refunds whose sum equals the breakdown total, never exceeds the captured balance under any combination in the test matrix, and whose rows carry `return_request_id`, reason `customer_return` and version-5 keys derived from the settlement key.
2. The same settlement submitted twice with the same key produces one set of refund rows and lines; another key on a settled request is refused; concurrent settlements produce one result.
3. No item with an unconfirmed exchange preference can be settled; the customer's choice (refund or wait) is recorded, reversible until settlement, reminded at 3, 7 and 14 days, and the request is marked on hold after 30 days with no automatic conversion.
4. Every deduction has a reason from the list, at least one staff photograph from the request, a compatible condition, is never on a fault line, and the combined deductions of a line never exceed 30 % of the accepted units' value; a settlement with deductions requires an accepted proposal or a lapsed window with no open dispute.
5. Per-unit values follow the deterministic rule: for every `q` ≤ 10, every `r` and every sequential split of partial returns, the partial values are non-decreasing in steps of `unit` or `unit + 1` and reconcile exactly to the discounted line value; Appendix A and the decision report's examples 1–10 pass as fixtures.
6. A COD settlement creates one pending manual refund with encrypted details and no `refunds` row; reveal requires an active `bank` grant, a fresh second factor and an explicit action, and writes an audit row; mark-paid is conditional, audited and e-mailed; unpaid rows alert daily from day 3 and escalate at day 10; ciphertext is nulled after retention; the rotation job re-encrypts every row and reports zero candidates afterwards.
7. The worker processes settlement refunds end to end through the fake gateway, including a lost response and a non-retryable refusal; a failed refund leaves the request closed and the customer page states the delay.
8. The customer page shows the proposal, the breakdown and each refund's state; the admin queue shows the cause; reports show refunds by cause, manual refunds and held units, from real rows only.
9. Every audit row for proposal, settlement, release, payment, cancellation, grant and reveal exists with the right outcome and no beneficiary data.
10. The complete gate passes locally and in CI; the Phase 6 and 8.1 suites run unchanged; migration and rollback rehearsed on a scratch database in the order 8.2 then 8.1.

## 10. Deployment, migration, rollback and operations

- **Secrets**: `CUSTOMER_DATA_KEY` provisioned before deploy; `_PREVIOUS` only during rotation; retired keys sealed for the backup retention window (Phase 11) and then destroyed with a recorded date.
- **Migration**: additive except the replaced grant CHECK; rehearse apply, rollback and re-apply on a scratch database; rollback order 8.2 before 8.1.
- **Runbooks to add**: proposing and settling a return; handling a dispute; paying a manual refund with a `bank` grant; rotating the key; what the sweep deletes; an on-hold exchange item.
- **Monitoring**: existing failed-refund alerts; new alerts for unanswered choices, open disputes, unpaid transfers and quarantine deadlines; the Operations page shows open obligations.
- **Operational risks**: staff proposing deductions without photographs (refused); owners forgetting transfers (alerts); a settlement on a COD order without bank details (refused with a message); key loss (unrecoverable details; the transfer is made by contacting the customer).
